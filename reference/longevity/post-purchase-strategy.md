# Longevity Post-Purchase Strategy: Provider-Amplified Hybrid Approach

> **Status:** Draft for leadership review
> **Date:** June 2026
> **Owner:** [Add owner]
> **Scope:** Master framework for post-purchase customer experience across the longevity therapy portfolio. Therapy-specific application briefs (e.g., NAD+, peptides) extend this framework.

---

## Executive Summary

**The clinical evidence supporting most longevity therapies — NAD+ precursors, peptides, and emerging interventions — is directionally promising but not substantial enough to anchor hard individual outcome claims.** Trials in this category are typically small (n=15–108), short (6–24 weeks), heterogeneous in design, and often funded or authored by manufacturers. The science confirms mechanisms — these therapies produce measurable biological changes. It does not confirm the specific lived experience we can guarantee individual customers.

This shapes the central tension in our post-purchase strategy: customers need product-specific reinforcement to survive the early evaluation window, but we cannot defensibly publish individual outcome promises across the portfolio.

**Recommendation:** Adopt the educational-identity framework (Approach B) as the strategic backbone, with selective interjections from the benefit-milestone framework (Approach A) that are anchored not in generalized claims but in our own provider network's interpretation of how the science applies to each individual. This is the only configuration that is both clinically defensible and emotionally resonant enough to drive LTV across our growing therapy portfolio. A detailed clinical evidence audit for NAD+ (the launch therapy) is included in [Appendix A](#appendix-a-nad-clinical-evidence-audit-example) as an illustrative example of how we'll audit each new therapy before launch.

## Framework Philosophy Comparison

| Dimension | Approach A: Benefit Milestones | Approach B: Educational Identity (GTM) |
|---|---|---|
| **Core premise** | Users stay because they feel the product working and can track progress against a timeline. | Users stay because they adopt a longevity identity and the brand becomes their trusted guide. |
| **Primary risk** | Overpromising. Clinical evidence supports population trends, not individual guarantees. Broken promises erode trust. | Underpromising. Pure education with no product reinforcement risks commoditization. User asks, "is this product doing anything?" |
| **Retention driver** | Sunk-cost + progress tracking: "I'm past the inflection point." | Identity + community: "I invest in longevity." |
| **Clinical defensibility** | Moderate-to-low. Individual benefit claims on a timeline are hard to defend without per-patient evidence. | High. Teaching longevity science doesn't make outcome promises. |
| **Best window** | Days 0–45: when user is evaluating "does this work for me?" | Day 45+: when user has decided to stay and needs ongoing reasons to remain engaged. |

## Recommended Hybrid: Provider-Amplified Educational Identity

**Approach B is the strategic backbone. Approach A is interjected at high-value moments, but the interjections are amplified through our provider network rather than published as marketing claims.**

This is the configuration that solves the central tension: customers need product-specific reinforcement to survive the early evaluation window, but we cannot defensibly publish individual outcome promises. The solution is to let providers translate the science into individualized guidance.

### How the provider layer changes the model

- **Studies become teaching tools, not promises.** Marketing references trials in aggregate ("clinical research shows NAD+ levels rise around week 4"). Providers then contextualize: "For someone with your profile, here's what the literature suggests we watch for." The promise is the conversation, not the outcome.
- **Provider interactions create defensible personalization.** A check-in with a licensed clinician at week 4 or month 3 is both a retention event and a clinical risk-management tool. It moves benefit framing from one-to-many marketing (legally risky) to one-to-one consultation (legally protected).
- **Anecdotal evidence finds its proper home.** Customer stories, Reddit-style experiential reports, and Trustpilot-style testimonials live inside the community and member-spotlight content (per the GTM brief), not in clinical claims. Providers reference them as "what we sometimes see" rather than "what you will experience."
- **The provider network becomes the differentiator.** Most supplement DTC brands compete on formulation and price. A provider-amplified post-purchase model competes on relationship and clinical guidance — a much harder moat to replicate.

## Tactical Messaging Plan

The eleven-touchpoint arc below is therapy-agnostic. Each touchpoint has a fixed timing, an internal name describing its messaging role, and a key message framed to apply across the portfolio. Therapy-specific application briefs (e.g., NAD+, peptides) fill in the support materials, channels, and clinical references that bring each touchpoint to life for a given product.

**Touchpoint groupings (for internal shorthand):**
- **Onboarding:** Welcome & onboarding, Science explainer
- **Milestones:** First week milestone, Clinical milestone check-in, Anniversary & what's next
- **Education & awareness:** Symptom awareness prompt, Consistency reminder, Lifestyle integration, Deeper science
- **Authority & belonging:** Provider perspective, Community & social proof

These groupings let us talk about clusters of touchpoints rather than individual ones — e.g., "all Education & awareness touchpoints go through medical review," "we're light on Milestones in months 2–5," "Authority & belonging touchpoints anchor the back half of the journey."

| Timing | Internal Name | Key Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 0–1** | Welcome & onboarding | You made a long-term investment in yourself. Here's how to use your therapy, what to expect, and how to reach us. |
| **Day 3–5** | Science explainer | Your body is adapting. Whether you feel something already or not yet — both are normal. Don't expect; observe. |
| **Day 7** | First week milestone | First week complete. Here's what your therapy is doing biologically. |
| **Week 2** | Symptom awareness prompt | Many people start paying attention to specific bodily signals around now. Worth observing, not expecting. |
| **Week 3** | Consistency reminder | You're approaching a meaningful clinical threshold. Stay consistent — consistency beats optimization. |
| **Week 4** | Clinical milestone check-in | Clinical research shows meaningful biological changes around now. You're past the foundation phase. |
| **Month 2** | Lifestyle integration | Your therapy is one piece of a longevity foundation. Sleep, nutrition, movement, and stress are the others. |
| **Month 3** | Provider perspective | Here's how clinicians think — about longevity, and about your therapy. |
| **Month 4** | Deeper science | Let's go deeper — biological age, recovery, metabolic health, advanced biomarkers. |
| **Month 5** | Community & social proof | You're part of something. Here's what other members are noticing and doing. |
| **Month 6** | Anniversary & what's next | Six months in. Here's what's emerging in longevity, and what's next for you. |

### Reading the arc

- **Days 0–7 are the foundation.** Set expectations, normalize early experience, and deliver mechanism education before any subjective claim.
- **Weeks 2–4 are the evaluation window.** Where most subscription churn happens. The Clinical milestone check-in at Week 4 is the highest-leverage touchpoint in the entire journey — the first time the provider layer actively engages.
- **Months 2–6 are the identity phase.** The customer has decided to stay; the work now is deepening the relationship and building toward what's next.

## Operational Requirements

- **Provider capacity model.** Define how many provider touchpoints per customer per year, what's automated vs. live, and where the cost lives. Async messaging at high scale; live consults at premium tiers.
- **Clinical content review.** All marketing claims pass through medical review before publication. Providers co-author key pieces so the science layer is theirs, not marketing's.
- **Tiered access.** Base tier gets provider-authored content + async check-ins. Premium tier gets live consultations, advanced biomarker testing, and personalized protocols. The provider relationship itself becomes the upsell.
- **Channel coordination.** Email, SMS, blog, app, and provider don't operate in silos. A single touchpoint at week 4 might fire across multiple channels simultaneously. This requires shared content briefs and orchestration tooling, not separate channel teams.
- **Per-therapy application briefs.** Each new therapy gets a dedicated brief (see the NAD+ Touchpoint Brief as the reference template) that translates the framework into therapy-specific messaging, support materials, channels, and clinical references.

---

## Why This Resolves the Tension

- **Clinically defensible.** We never publish an individual outcome promise. Clinical literature is cited as "what research shows," not "what you'll feel." Provider conversations carry the individualization — which is exactly what clinical practice is for.
- **Retention-effective.** The provider relationship itself is the strongest LTV driver. A customer with a clinician they trust doesn't churn the way a customer with just a shipping cadence churns.
- **GTM-aligned.** Approach B's educational structure (Months 1–6 themes) is preserved as the backbone. Approach A's milestone moments are repurposed as provider interjection opportunities, not as marketing claims.
- **Portfolio-scalable.** The same eleven-touchpoint arc works for NAD+, peptides, and future therapies. We invest once in the framework and orchestration; each new therapy gets its own application brief without rebuilding the architecture.
- **Differentiated.** Almost no DTC longevity competitor has a real provider relationship layer. It's a meaningful moat.

## Appendix A: NAD+ Clinical Evidence Audit (Example)

> **Note:** This audit is specific to NAD+, the launch therapy. It is included here as an illustrative example of the evidence audit we will conduct for every new therapy before launch. Each audit informs how messaging is framed in that therapy's application brief.

Honest assessment of the nine human trials commonly cited in NAD+ marketing. Full citations in [Appendix B](#appendix-b-references).

| Trial | n | Wks | COI Risk | What it actually shows | Provider verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Yi et al. (2022)** — Dose-dependent RCT | 80 | 8.5 | 🟢 Low | NAD+ rises with dose; modest walking distance & SF-36 gains; biological age stable vs. placebo | Best-designed in the set. ~20 per arm. Supports "NAD+ goes up," not lifestyle outcome promises. |
| **Huang / Uthever (2022)** — Manufacturer-led RCT | 66 | 8.5 | 🔴 HIGH | 38% NAD+/NADH increase by day 60 | Author employed by Effepharm (Uthever manufacturer). Used as core marketing asset. Providers will flag immediately. |
| **Liao et al. (2021)** — Amateur runner RCT | 48 | 6 | 🟢 Low | Improved ventilatory threshold at 600–1200 mg. No change in VO2max, peak power, O2-pulse. | Independent team. Narrow finding — headline "aerobic capacity" overstates it. 12 per arm, 2 women per arm. |
| **Kim et al. (2022)** — Sleep & fatigue RCT | 108 | 12 | 🟢 Low | PSQI sleep score NOT improved. Afternoon drowsiness & sit-to-stand improved in PM-dose group only. | Best-powered trial here. Primary endpoint missed. "NMN improves sleep" is a broad mischaracterization. |
| **Katayoshi et al. (2023)** — Blood markers RCT | 30 | 12 | 🟢 Low | Blood NAD+ rises starting week 4, returns to baseline 4 wks post-cessation | Useful for timeline framing of NAD+ pharmacokinetics. Biomarker only — no clinical outcome. |
| **Yoshino et al. (2021)** — Insulin sensitivity | 25 | 10 | 🟡 Medium | ~25% improvement in muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic postmenopausal women only | Published in *Science*. Two authors hold NMN-use patents. Narrow population — doesn't generalize. |
| **Nature Metabolism (2025)** — 14-day comparison | 65 | 2 | 🟢 Low | NR & NMN both raise circulatory NAD+ in 2 weeks | Pharmacokinetic confirmation, not clinical efficacy. Confirms mechanism only. |
| **Mass General (2025)** — Long COVID RCT | 58 | 24 | 🟢 Low | Cognition primary endpoint MISSED. Exploratory sub-analyses suggest fatigue/sleep/mood gains. | Negative primary result. Exploratory findings are hypothesis-generating only. Cannot cite as proof. |
| **Fukumoto et al. (2025)** — Hair quality | 15 | 12 | 🟡 Medium | Hair density & diameter increase in single-arm trial | Single-arm, no placebo. Case series, not RCT. No provider would cite as clinical evidence. |

> **Bottom line for NAD+:** What we can defensibly claim — NAD+ precursor supplementation is safe, well-tolerated, and elevates blood NAD+ levels in humans. The FDA confirmed NMN's lawful supplement status in September 2025. What we cannot defensibly claim — any specific individual-outcome promise tied to a timeline. The trials are too small, too short, and too heterogeneous to support guarantees. **This same audit pattern should be applied to every new therapy before launch.**

## Appendix B: References

### B.1 NAD+ Clinical Trials Cited

**Yi et al. (2022) — 60-Day Dose-Dependent RCT (n=80)**
GeroScience. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, four-arm trial. 300/600/900mg NMN daily. Significant NAD+ elevation at day 30 and day 60 (p≤0.001). Walking distance improvement, biological age stabilization, SF-36 health score gains.
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36482258/
- Full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11357-022-00705-1

**Huang (2022) — Uthever RCT (n=66)**
Frontiers in Aging. Double-blind, parallel-design RCT. 300mg NMN daily for 60 days. NAD+/NADH +11.3% day 30, +38% day 60 vs. 14.3% placebo. Author employed by Effepharm (Uthever manufacturer).
- Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging/articles/10.3389/fragi.2022.851698/full
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35821806/

**Liao et al. (2021) — 6-Week Aerobic Capacity RCT (n=48 runners)**
Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Four-arm dose-response design. 600–1200mg/day NMN significantly improved ventilatory threshold but NOT VO2max, peak power, or O2-pulse.
- PMC: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8265078/

**Kim et al. (2022) — 12-Week Sleep Quality RCT (n=108)**
Nutrients. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, time-of-day arms. 250mg NMN for 12 weeks. PSQI primary endpoint NOT met. Afternoon dosing reduced drowsiness and improved sit-to-stand.
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8877443/

**Katayoshi et al. (2023) — 12-Week NAD+ Pharmacokinetics (n=30)**
Scientific Reports. Double-blind, placebo-controlled. 250mg NMN for 12 weeks. Blood NAD+ significantly elevated starting at week 4, returned to baseline 4 weeks after cessation.
- DOAJ: https://doaj.org/article/2f8d89e369a942b6885b4956c5b26a28

**Yoshino et al. (2021) — 10-Week Insulin Sensitivity (n=25)**
Science (AAAS). 250mg NMN daily in prediabetic postmenopausal women with overweight/obesity. ~25% improvement in muscle insulin sensitivity. Two authors hold patents on NMN use.

**Nature Metabolism (2025) — 14-Day Precursor Comparison (n=65)**
Randomized, open-label, placebo-controlled. NR and NMN comparably increased circulatory NAD+ in 14 days. Pharmacokinetic confirmation.
- Nature: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-025-01421-8

**Mass General / eClinicalMedicine (2025) — 24-Week Long-COVID RCT (n=58)**
Lancet eClinicalMedicine. 2000mg/day NR for 20 weeks. Cognition primary endpoint not met. Exploratory analyses suggested fatigue, sleep, and mood gains in subset.
- Lancet: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(25)00567-X/fulltext

**Fukumoto et al. (2025) — 12-Week Hair Quality (n=15)**
Cosmetics. Single-arm pre-post design, no placebo. NMN increased hair density (55.9 to 87.7 hairs/cm²) and shaft diameter. Case-series level evidence only.
- DOAJ: https://doaj.org/article/c2a6290139ac4fcc8239bdfd52808580

### B.2 Critical Reviews & Meta-Analyses

- **ScienceDirect (2026):** Critical review concluding NMN trials are too small, short, and heterogeneous to provide more than initial indications of possible benefits. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889157526001705
- **Frontiers in Pharmacology (2024):** Review noting NMN clinical evidence is limited in quantity and scale. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1436597/full
- **Tandfonline meta-analysis:** 12 acceptable human RCTs identified for glucose/lipid metabolism. Funnel plot analysis suggests possible publication bias. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10408398.2024.2387324

### B.4 Anecdotal Source Caveats

- **Trustpilot reviews:** Cited reviews come from NAD+ At Home (nadplusathome.com), a single UK brand. Useful for language/sentiment patterns; significant selection bias. Not independent evidence. https://www.trustpilot.com/review/nadplusathome.com
- **WebMD reviews:** 46+ self-reported, unverified ratings on the Nicotinamide Riboside ingredient page. Useful for recurring theme identification only. https://reviews.webmd.com/vitamins-supplements/ingredientreview-1560-NICOTINAMIDE-RIBOSIDE
- **Reddit communities:** r/NMN, r/Longevity, r/Supplements, r/Biohackers contain active discussion. Individual threads typically low-engagement (5–50 upvotes). Value is in meta-pattern recognition across many threads, not in citing specific posts. The audience skews male and younger than likely NAD+ customer profile.
